01 / SOLAR BASICS 5 MIN READ

Solar panel basics: How rooftop solar works.

Rooftop solar converts sunlight into electricity that can be used in your home. Understanding the basic components makes it easier to compare systems and make informed decisions.

Understanding the main components

Solar panels contain photovoltaic cells that convert sunlight into direct-current electricity. An inverter converts this electricity into alternating current, which is used by most household appliances.

A typical home solar setup may also include mounting structures, monitoring equipment, electrical protection devices and, depending on the design, battery storage.

How does it work?

Sunlight reaches the panels, electricity is generated, and the inverter converts it into a form your home can use.

What should you check first?

  • Your average monthly electricity consumption.
  • The usable area available on your roof.
  • Shade from nearby trees or buildings.
  • Your preference for battery backup, if needed.

Start by reviewing your electricity bills and arranging a roof assessment. These provide a useful foundation for choosing a suitable system.

02 / INSTALLATION 7 MIN READ

What happens during a solar installation?

A successful solar installation begins with understanding your energy requirements and evaluating your property. Knowing the stages helps you prepare for the process.

The installation process

  1. Energy assessment: Your electricity consumption and energy requirements are reviewed.
  2. Site inspection: Roof space, shade, orientation and structural conditions are assessed.
  3. System design: The suitable system capacity and equipment are selected.
  4. Installation: Panels, mounting structures, inverter and electrical connections are installed.
  5. Testing and activation: The system is tested and applicable approvals and connection processes are completed.
Before installation

Request a written proposal that explains the equipment, warranties, installation scope, costs and expected timelines.

Questions to ask your installer

  • What equipment will be used?
  • What warranties are included?
  • Who handles approvals and documentation?
  • What support is available after installation?

Clear communication helps you understand what is included and what to expect at every stage.

03 / SAVINGS AND COSTS 6 MIN READ

Understanding solar savings and payback.

Solar savings depend on electricity generation, household consumption, electricity tariffs and the applicable rules for your connection.

What influences your savings?

The size of your solar system, available sunlight, roof direction, shade and equipment performance all influence how much electricity is generated.

Your household usage pattern also matters. Using electricity during daylight may allow you to consume more of the electricity generated by your own system.

Understanding payback

Estimated payback compares the net initial system cost with the expected annual savings. It is an estimate, not a guaranteed result.

Compare solar proposals carefully

  • Compare the same system capacity and scope.
  • Review equipment specifications and warranties.
  • Ask how estimated generation was calculated.
  • Consider maintenance and financing costs.

You can also use our solar savings calculator for an initial illustration. A site-specific estimate is important before making a decision.

04 / SYSTEM CARE 4 MIN READ

A practical guide to solar panel maintenance.

Solar panels are designed for outdoor conditions. However, periodic monitoring and appropriate maintenance can help you identify issues early.

Simple maintenance practices

  • Monitor your system's electricity generation.
  • Check for visible dirt or debris build-up.
  • Watch for unexpected drops in performance.
  • Keep nearby vegetation from creating shade.
  • Maintain your service and warranty records.
Safety is important

Do not climb onto your roof or handle electrical equipment to clean or inspect your system. Use qualified professionals when required.

When should you request service?

Contact your installer if the inverter displays a fault, monitoring shows a persistent unexpected drop in generation, or you notice visible damage to cables or equipment.

05 / BUYING GUIDE 6 MIN READ

How to choose the right solar system size.

The right solar system should match your electricity consumption, available roof space, budget and the rules that apply to your property.

Start with your electricity usage

Review several months of electricity bills to understand your consumption. Consider whether you may add high-consumption appliances or electric vehicle charging in the future.

Important factors to consider

  • Your monthly electricity consumption.
  • The usable space and condition of your roof.
  • Sunlight exposure and surrounding shade.
  • Panel and inverter specifications.
  • Battery backup requirements, if applicable.
  • Warranty and after-sales support.
Compare before deciding

Ask installers to explain the assumptions behind their energy-generation estimates. Comparable proposals make decisions easier.

A qualified site assessment can help translate your electricity usage and roof conditions into a suitable system recommendation.

06 / SOLAR MYTHS 5 MIN READ

Six common solar energy myths explained.

Solar energy is widely used, but misconceptions can make it difficult to understand what a residential system can realistically provide.

Myth 01: Solar panels only work in hot weather.

Solar panels need sunlight, not high temperatures. Heat can actually affect panel efficiency.

Myth 02: Solar panels generate electricity at night.

Panels do not generate electricity without sunlight. Battery storage can provide previously stored energy.

Myth 03: Every roof is suitable for solar.

Roof condition, shade, orientation, available area and structural considerations all matter.

Myth 04: A larger system is always better.

System capacity should be matched to your energy needs and applicable connection rules.

Myth 05: Solar panels never need maintenance.

Monitoring and occasional maintenance checks are useful for identifying problems.

Myth 06: Every home gets the same savings.

Savings vary depending on usage, generation, electricity tariffs and local policies.

The best solar decisions come from reliable information and an assessment of your own home.
